# Zones: the safe zone, the one map and its teleport points

*Built in roadmap items E-2.9b (the safe zone), CT-2.16 and E-2.76a to E-2.76d (the one open map and its teleport points), E-2.41 (the soul gate, no longer placed) and CT-2.72 (the starting forest removed). The safe zone is the settled rule GDD 6.1; one open map per server is the settled rule GDD 5.7. A new character's opening, the starter cave, has its own page: [The starter cave](soul-cave.md).*

## What you see

A new character does not start on the map: it starts alone in its own starter cave and climbs a sewer ladder into the town once it holds a soul ([The starter cave](soul-cave.md)). The grate it comes out at, `SewerGrate`, stands on the town square west of the plaza well.

Inside the town, or any place marked as safe, nothing can hurt you and you can hurt nothing. Press an ability key and nothing happens; a monster's swing does not land; a projectile that reaches you pops harmlessly. Monsters outside stop caring about you the moment you step in, and pick you up again when you step out. If you wear a soul (the human), you show its body while you are inside and your primary's body again once you step out (see [Souls](soul-model.md)).

The world is one map: the town in the middle and four fields around it, the Beach to the east, the Fields to the west, the Jungle to the south and the Pass to the north. You walk out of a town gate into a field and nothing loads, nothing teleports, nothing is written to the log. A teleport point is a one-way door; on the map today the only ones are in the starter cave, which is a level of its own. A teleport moves you; if you joined someone else's game you may stand still for a moment first, with a card that says "Travelling", until your own machine has the ground around you; then you walk.

## How it works, in plain words

**The soul gate** (`ASoulGate`, E-2.41) still exists as a class but none is placed: the one that stood before the town went with the starting forest (CT-2.72; the D-2.29 memo retires it). It is one actor: a wall of collision every pawn hits, an invisible trigger in front of it, and a door mesh. When a player touches the trigger the server checks whether that player wears (or, with `bRequireWorn` off, owns) the gate's soul. The first who does opens it for everyone for the rest of the session: one replicated flag drops the wall and hides the door. There is no key item and no per-player state.

**The safe zone** is a placed box. The server marks every pawn inside it, player or monster, and clears the mark when it leaves; a pawn standing in two overlapping zones stays marked until it has left both. The mark is one replicated flag on the pawn, and every combat rule reads that flag rather than asking the box:

- a move pressed while marked is refused on the server before anything plays, and the owning client refuses it locally too so no request goes out;
- a hit whose target or attacker is marked is not applied, with no flinch and no miss roll;
- a projectile that reaches a marked pawn pops without a hit;
- a monster never picks a marked pawn as its target and drops one that becomes marked.

Death cannot happen inside, so the death penalty never applies there; "no loss" follows from "no combat".

**The one map** is `Content/Levels/Lvl_Slice_WP`, a World Partition map (GDD 5.7): the town and the four fields laid out on one landscape in grey box (walls, path strips, landmark towers; `docs/levels/README.md`), the town at the centre, each field joined to a town gate by open ground. One light rig, one navmesh, one safe zone over the town. The one PlayerStart stands in the town south-west of the Town well, the spawn of a character with no saved place when the starter cave is switched off. The starting forest, its grey box, its pickups, Walter's old body, the dead Creepy, the soul gate and the forest's landmark tower were removed from the map by `scripts/editor/remove_starting_forest.py` (CT-2.72); the town's south-west cut wall and the destination `FromForest` inside it stay, so an old record whose checkpoint is `FromForest` still comes back in town. The engine streams the ground around each player by itself, in cells; the server keeps the whole map resident (server streaming is off, the engine's default), so a player can be moved anywhere on it at any time and the ground is already there for the server. The map was built new rather than converted, because the engine's convert commandlet stops on a landscape that lives in a sublevel and the old landscape held nothing worth keeping (`docs/plans/level-changes-2026-09-22.md`). It is the map the game opens: `GameDefaultMap` and `EditorStartupMap` in `Config/DefaultEngine.ini` name it and the packaging command in `docs/playtest.md` cooks it alone (the cook writes the streaming cells itself). The town keeps its cobble cube with its top 2 cm above the landscape until painted layers exist (CT-2.17); Zone1's grass cube is gone, the landscape is its floor.

**What a safe zone reads (2026-10-05).** A safe zone does not overlap the pawn's capsule. Every pawn carries a small sphere of fixed size, the zone probe, on a collision channel of its own, and the safe zone's box overlaps that channel alone. The capsule changes size with the body that is worn, and inside a town a player shows the worn human while outside they show their primary soul, so at a zone's edge a large primary's capsule reached into the zone the moment the body swapped, the flag flipped, the body swapped back, and so on until the stack ran out (the crash of 2026-10-05, a Gargoyle leaving the town). The probe does not change with the body, so one crossing is one `entered` or one `left`. The region toast and the arena volume still read the capsule and may flicker at an edge with a large body.

**Wells stand on the ground (2026-10-05).** A teleport destination sits about a pawn's height above its ground, and its marker mesh (the well) used to hang at the actor's own height plus its offset. At BeginPlay the destination now traces down from 500 uu above itself to 3,000 uu below on the floor channel and seats the marker on what it hits; while the ground under it has not streamed in yet it looks again on a timer rather than leaving the well in the air.

## Moving around the map

Most of the map you walk. A **teleport point** (`ATeleportPoint`) is a box a player walks into; it names a **teleport destination** (`ATeleportDestination`) by a tag, and destinations are never streamed out, so the server can always find them. On the one map today there is no teleport point; the starter cave holds two, its drop into the arena and the sewer ladder, whose destination `SewerGrate` stands in town. A point can refuse a character that owns no soul yet (`bRequiresSoul`, the ladder's) and tells them why. Walk into a point and the server moves you to the destination on its next poll, standing still and facing the way the destination faces. A walk you had clicked ends the moment you touch the point, so you cannot walk on into the same point again (E-2.64). If someone is already standing on the destination, you land on a free spot a step or two around it instead; only when every spot is blocked are you set down on the destination itself and the characters shuffle apart, so a crowd at a door never leaves anyone behind (E-2.67).

If you joined someone else's game, your machine streams the cells around your new position only after the server has moved you there. Until then you would be standing on ground your machine does not have yet, so you are held: the server turns your movement off until your machine reports the cell under your feet, and your own machine holds itself from the moment the travel starts until it has landed and the engine says your cells have streamed, or until ten seconds have passed, whichever comes first (E-2.57, `ArrivalTimeoutSeconds`). The card that says "Travelling" is that hold: it shows while you are travelling and goes away when your machine lets you walk. Everyone else sees you go. Your bag, souls, gems, level and experience travel with you because they live on your character.

The place you last arrived at is your checkpoint. Since E-2.132 the death screen says which: the Checkpoint button reads "Checkpoint: " and the name of the region the checkpoint stands in ("where you started" before your first arrival or bind); the server sends the checkpoint's tag to its owner only. If you die and pick the checkpoint, you come back at that destination; if you have never taken a teleport, you come back at the level's PlayerStart. If you pick the town, you come back at the town's destination, `SewerGrate` (E-2.46, `TownDestination` in `Config/DefaultGame.ini`); inside the starter cave the town button is greyed and a town request goes to the cave's start instead. Since E-2.76e a destination can also be flagged as a **checkpoint**: walk within about three metres of it and it becomes your checkpoint without any teleport, and the first time you bind it your Health and Mana fill up; stepping out and back in does nothing, so it cannot be used as a free heal. A checkpoint is not a safe zone: monsters can stand on it and hit you there. The host log says `checkpoint: <pawn> bound <tag> (vitals filled)` when it happens. This follows the D-2.12 memo's recommendation, provisionally, behind `Slime.Checkpoints`; what a checkpoint looks like (a well, a crystal, the gazebo), whether it heals and where they stand are still design's (D-2.12), and which destinations carry the flag on the map is placed by hand in the editor. Since E-2.113 a destination also carries an optional **marker mesh** (`Marker`, a static mesh set per placed destination, standing `MarkerRelativeLocation` from the landing spot, behind it by default so nobody lands inside it; it blocks walking and cuts the navmesh like any placed mesh), so a checkpoint well is one placed actor: a tag, the checkpoint flag and `SM_Well1` on the marker. Jon's rule (2026-09-24): a well at the entrance of every zone, more wells before a boss fight or at a dungeon door as that content is built; tags read `<Region>` at the entrance, `<Region>Lair` before a boss, `<Region>Dungeon<Name>` at a dungeon door, with a map prefix on a second map. On the map today the entrance destinations carry the flag and the well: `Town` on the square beside the shrine, and `Beach` at the Eastgate Dunes east of the town (the old `FromTown`, retagged when the beach blockout replaced Zone1, CT-2.27; the town sits at the origin since the footprint pass, CT-2.25, with the forest un-rotated to its south-west and four gates in the wall); the four lair destinations (`BeachLair`, `FieldsLair`, `JungleLair`, `PassLair`) carry the flag and the well too since the art passes of 2026-09-25 (CT-2.32, CT-2.33), each standing outside its arena wall; the two new ones, like every placed destination, are never streamed out (`bIsSpatiallyLoaded` off). The well's look is still the memo's placeholder until Duilio records D-2.12. Which places get teleport points is design's too (D-8.6 memo §8.6).

**What is gone.** Everything the old door-streamed zones did is gone with them: there are no zone sublevels, no zone name on your character, no "Entering <zone>" card, no zone loaded before the first player spawns, no zone kept loaded because players join into it, no zone unloaded half a minute after its last player leaves, no per-player loading and dropping of zones, and no distinction between the host's view and a joiner's beyond the arrival hold above. A late join cannot fall into an unloaded starting zone any more, because there is no zone to unload: the engine never streams a PlayerStart out, and the server holds the whole map. The old maps (`Lvl_Slice`, `StartingForest`, `StartingTown`, `Zone1`) stay in the project uncooked until the content change that deletes them; nothing plays them, and the code that streamed them no longer exists. The four old map files themselves (`Lvl_Slice`, `StartingForest`, `StartingTown`, `Zone1`) and the Paragon archer pack, whose last user was the old forest map, were removed from the project on 2026-10-13.

## The town portal (E-2.277, 2026-11-04)

Out in the world, press **T** (rebindable on the options page as Town portal) and stand still. After two and a half seconds a portal opens where you stood, and its twin opens in front of the Town well. Walk into either one's glowing disc and you come out at the other, through the same travel as any teleport, so a joiner is held for a moment while their machine streams the town in. Each end is labelled "Portal to town" or "Portal back" with the owner's name over it. You land a couple of metres short of the disc, so you never bounce straight back.

The channel breaks, with no portal, if you walk more than about 30 cm, use a move, or take a hit that deals damage. You cannot open one in town, in the arena ring, in the starter cave (its one way out is the ladder), while dead or while travelling; you are told why. It costs nothing and has no cooldown. You have one pair at a time: it closes when you have gone through to town and back once, when you open another, when you leave the game, or after ten minutes. With the server's PvP rule off anyone may use your portal, with it on only you; monsters never follow. A portal is never your checkpoint: dying still returns you to your well or the town.

Built on the server: `UPortalComponent` on the pawn runs the request (`ServerRequestOpenPortal`, under the flask's rate row) and the channel on a timer; `APlayerPortal` is a teleport destination the server spawns and replicates for its look. Numbers in `UPortalSettings` (`Config/DefaultGame.ini`). Provisional on the D-2.20 memo's option (a), behind `Slime.TownPortal`; nothing is saved, so an open portal does not survive a restart. For testers: `Slime.Portal`, `Slime.ShowPortals`, `Slime.PortalStep field|town`. While the channel runs, a bar above the console counts the seconds down ("Opening portal 2.4 s") and its blue fill empties toward the left. Each end stands as an upright blue oval facing the camera, with a light and its label above it. Since #594 (2026-11-04) the oval wears a material of its own, `M_PortalGate` under `Content/Art/VFX/Kit/`, made by `scripts/editor/portal_gate_material.py` with the editor closed: translucent, unlit and two-sided, drawn from the disc's UVs with no texture, a deep blue body turning in two spiral arms, a bright rim just inside a clean edge and a pale core. Its colours, glow and swirl speed are `GateColour`, `GateRimColour`, `GateGlow` and `GateSwirlSpeed` in `UPortalSettings`, and `GateMaterial` names it; with `GateMaterial` unset the gate falls back to the aura smoke of the first build. The label stands 120 uu above the oval's top.

## The town gates open by level (E-2.279, 2026-11-08)

The East gate to the Beach is open to everyone. The other three carry a plate beside the opening and a thin grey-box slab in it (2026-11-09; before that a level over the opening, which the raised door hid, and a block as deep as the wall). The plate reads the field's name, and under it, only while the gate is shut to you, "Locked: unlocked at level 4" at the West gate to the Fields, 6 at the South gate to the Jungle, 8 at the North gate to the Pass; open to you, the name alone (Jon, 2026-11-09: the level shown to everyone was confusing; the plate reads with your own door, so two friends at one gate may read it differently). Below that level the door is down, filling the opening, and walking at it you stop against it while the notice row says "Level 6 needed to enter the Jungle (South gate)", once (the exit named since 2026-11-09); it says it again only if you walk away from the gate and come back (Jon, 2026-11-08: "once per approach"). Reach the level, by a kill or a level-up right there at the gate, and within a second the door lifts clear of the opening (a short move, about half a second, no sound) and you walk through. The door is yours alone: your game draws each gate from your own character's level, so a level 8 friend sees the Pass gate raised and walks through it while, beside them, a level 3 friend sees the same gate down and is stopped; as the friend reaches the gate, the level 3 friend's door rises at once and stays up while the friend is in the doorway, however long, and a moment after they are through (a second and a half, 2026-11-09), then lowers again, so nobody is seen walking through a shut door (Jon, 2026-11-08: "grey-box gate that is lowered when quest state not met and raised when player can enter the zone"; the only requirement today is the level). Nobody can carry anyone through. Nothing ever pushes you back out of a field, and a character never loses a level, so a gate you have passed stays passed; coming back toward the town you always pass, and the door is up for you while you do. A well behind a gate (the Fields well and its lair well, and so on) binds only a player the gate would let through, so nobody respawns in a field it could not walk into. Monsters never come through a town gate, in either direction, whatever their level: the wall stands for every monster, and only a player's own pawn is ever let past it (Jon, 2026-11-08: "monsters stay out"). The levels are the D-2.31 memo's placeholders (each the zone's lowest Normal) and the playtest checks them; the door is a grey slab until art, and its thickness and the moment it stays up for a passing friend are `FieldGateDoorThickness` and `FieldGateCourtesySeconds` in `DefaultGame.ini`.

For testers: `Slime.FieldGates 0` (or `-dpcvars=Slime.FieldGates=0` at launch) opens every exit as before and hides the labels and the doors. `Slime.SetLevel 4` while standing at the West gate lifts the door and lets you through within a second. The host log says `FieldGate <name>: <pawn> refused (level 3, gate 4)` once per approach, `... passes (level 4, gate 4)` or `... passes (level 3, gate 4, leaving the field)`, and `checkpoint: Fields not bound by <pawn> (level 3; the West gate needs 4)` at a guarded well; every machine that draws logs its own door, `FieldGate <name>: door lowered for <pawn> (level 1, gate 4)` and `door raised for <pawn> (level 4, gate 4)`, so a host and a joiner at one gate log different doors. The gates are `FieldGate_West`, `FieldGate_South` and `FieldGate_North` in the `Town` folder of the Outliner, placed by `scripts/editor/place_field_gates.py`; their levels are rows of `Data/DT_FieldGates.csv`, never on the actor.

For engineers: `Zones/SoulGate.*` in its level mode (`GateRow` set): the gate reads its `DT_FieldGates` row once at BeginPlay (`Zones/FieldGateTable.*`, `FFieldGateRow`: `FieldName`, `GateLevel`, `Wells`; the path is `FieldGatesTable` in `Config/DefaultGame.ini`). The blocker is one box that blocks every pawn; the per-pawn pass is `UPrimitiveComponent::IgnoreActorWhenMoving(gate, true)` on the pawn's capsule, set by `JudgePawn` from the trigger overlap on every machine (the server for every pawn, a client for its own pawn and the proxies it smooths; a player's pawn is told from a monster by its controller or its player state, and the level is replicated to all), so the server's replay and the client's prediction agree and no correction pulls the pawn back. A pass by level holds for the session (a level is never lost); a pass for leaving alone is kept in `LeavingInside` and ends (`IgnoreActorWhenMoving(gate, false)`) once the pawn is out of the trigger box, read by position in gate space (`EvaluateLeavePassEnds`), since a capsule that ignores the gate is left out of the trigger's overlap queries too; so an under-level pawn that came out cannot turn and walk back in. A refused pawn is kept in `RefusedInside`, one entry per approach, and looked at once a second (`CheckRefusedInside`, a timer, no tick) until it leaves the trigger or its level reaches the gate's; the notice goes by `AMonsterCharacter::ClientNotice` from the authority once per entry (`FRefusedPawn::bTold`, `EvaluateNoticeDue`), and the entry is dropped once the pawn is out of the trigger, so the next arrival is told again. Only a pawn `EvaluateJudged` admits (a player controller on this machine, or a player state) is judged at all; a field monster is neither, so it never gets the per-pawn pass and the wall stands for it with the switch on (with the switch off the wall is down for everyone, as before E-2.279, and what keeps monsters out of the town is the safe zone, not the gate). `IsOnFieldSide` reads the pawn's side from the gate's +X, which the placing script points into the field. The door is the `Door` mesh component, given on a machine that draws (`GetNetMode() != NM_DedicatedServer`) the layout settings' `FieldGateDoorMesh` (the engine cube) in `FieldGateDoorMaterial` (`MI_Greybox_GateShut`), scaled to the blocker's extent (`SetUpDoor`), when the placed actor set none; `RefreshDoor` reads the first local player controller's pawn (`GetViewerPawn`: a client's own, the listen host's own) against the row through the pure `EvaluateDoorRaised` (no pawn reads lowered; a leaving pass reads raised; the switch off reads raised and the door is hidden anyway), twice a second by `DoorLookTimer` and at once when that pawn is judged or its leaving pass ends, and on a change starts `DoorMoveTimer` (30 steps a second, only while moving) along `EvaluateDoorLift` over `FieldGateDoorSeconds` (0.6 s), lifting the door by the blocker's full height; a move that turns round carries on from where the door is. Nothing of the door replicates and the door has no collision. The pure rules are `EvaluateLevelPass`, `EvaluateWellBindAllowed`, `EvaluateLeavePassEnds`, `EvaluateNoticeDue`, `EvaluateJudged`, `EvaluateDoorRaised`, `EvaluateDoorLift` and `FormatRefusal` (tests `SandboxARPG.Zones.FieldGate`, six). The well rule sits in `ATeleportDestination::HandleBindOverlap` through `UFieldGateTable::FindGuarding(tag)`; D-2.15 well travel is not built, and the same lookup is where it would refuse a destination. The switch is `Slime.FieldGates`, read by `ApplyOpenState` at BeginPlay and at every touch of the trigger. The soul mode (`GateSoul`) is unchanged.

## The starter cave

A new character starts in a copy of the starter cave of its own, a separate level loaded beside the map at runtime, and climbs its sewer ladder into the town at `SewerGrate` once it holds a soul. Its rooms, its monsters, how a copy is loaded for one player and unloaded behind them, and its death rules are on [The starter cave](soul-cave.md). Provisional on the D-2.29 memo.

## Region names on entry

Since E-2.115 (2026-09-25) each region of the map carries an `ARegionZone`, a box with a display name. When your own pawn walks into one, its name shows on the toast card at the top of the screen for a few seconds ("Zone 1: The Beach"), the area banner Diablo and Path of Exile show; the log says `RegionZone <name>: <pawn> entered '<name>'`. It is cosmetic and local: nothing replicates and the server never reads it. The names are the remake plan's placeholders until the board is transcribed (D-0.2). All four town gates were open from CT-2.34 (2026-10-03) until E-2.279 (2026-11-08) put a level on three of them (below); what opens a gate in the real game is still D-8.7's and D-2.31's. The starter cave carries its own region, "Soul Cave".

## The arena ring

Outside the town's south gate stands the arena ring. Since E-2.145 (2026-09-28) any players standing inside it can hurt each other, whatever the server's PvP setting: two, three or four at once, anyone who walks in, not only the pair the queue sends there. Step out and the server's setting applies again: a player outside the ring cannot hit one inside it on a PvP-off server, and the other way round. Name plates turn red for another player while both of you are inside. Monsters are unaffected, and dying in the ring costs what dying anywhere costs. The ring's bounds are a box (`PvPArenaVolume`, labelled `PvPArena_South` in the `Town/Arena` folder of the Outliner) drawn as a red outline in the editor and moved or resized by hand (`Extent`). The host log says `Arena <box>: <pawn> entered` and `left`. This is Jon's rule taken provisionally until Duilio answers D-2.14 (memo section 8), behind `bArenaPvP` in `Config/DefaultGame.ini`; what a match is, whether anything is won or lost, and whether the ring moves inside the walls are still design's.

## Ground materials

Every floor in the slice wears one of two instances of a game-owned master material, `M_GroundTile` (`Content/Art/Materials/Ground/`). The master tiles in world space, through the pack's `MF_WorldCoords-XY`, so the landscape and a scaled floor cube show the same texture size without any UV work. It holds two texture sets, each one call of the material function `MF_GroundSet` (base colour, normal, an optional AORM map), and mixes them by a world-space noise. The parameters are `TileSize`, `TileScaleB` (set B tiles at a multiple of set A), `BlendThreshold`, `BlendContrast`, `BlendStrength`, `NoiseScale`, `Roughness` and `Tint`.

- `MI_Ground_Grass`: grass over dirt, dirt only in the noise peaks (threshold 0.68, contrast 6). On the landscape of `Lvl_Slice_WP`, the floor of the fields.
- `MI_Ground_Cobble`: the same stone in both sets, set B at 1.6 times the size, blended softly, so no two tiles line up. On the town's ground since 2026-11-09: a plaza of twenty tiles over the well, the shrine, the bench, the keeper and the grate, and a cobble road from the plaza through each of the four gates to its well, laid by `scripts/editor/dress_town.py` over the grey box (the two grey path strips took the same stone). The floor cube this line named before is gone; the fields around stay grass. The same evening `scripts/editor/dress_town_market.py` set the instance's `Tint` to (0.58, 0.54, 0.48), since the untinted stone measured a quarter brighter than the grass under the pinned exposure, grew the square by a ring of sixteen tiles to x -1500..1450, y -1500..1300, and dressed it (Jon: "a marketplace in the central square, trees, mostly coble stone but some small areas with dirt/soil around the trees"): six Provencal trees (four `SM_Tree_01`, two `SM_Tree_Cypress`), each blocking at the trunk only through a hidden cylinder, each over two soil decals; three MedCastle `SM_A1Booth` stalls in a north-west market court with a lane between them and the plaza, a forked post on a ring of stones at the lane's west end and two posts with a beam over its mouth on the North road (the stalls and posts block, the rest is dressing); five smaller worn discs where the market's feet go. The soil is `M_GroundDecal` under `Content/Art/Materials/Ground/`, a deferred decal of the Provencal dirt (`T_Dirt_B`, its normal `T_Dirt_M`) tiled in world space with a soft round edge wandered by noise at the cobble's blend scale, instanced as `MI_GroundDecal_Soil` and the darker `MI_GroundDecal_Worn`; a decal paints the stone under it, so no plane, no step and no collision. The plan and the placement table are `builds/pt1109/polish-town2-layout.md` and `polish-town2-plan.md` (not in the repo). Later the same night `scripts/editor/dress_town_square.py` finished the list (Jon: "do all now"): the market's furniture and goods (haywagon, carts, a wicker pen, crates, sacks, barrels, jars), benches under the far trees, two benches and a stool at the well over a worn disc, bushes, tufts and small stones at the soil patches and along the cobble's grass edge; eighteen `ALightProp` lights with the NFire_00 flame and no shadows (a torch pair on each gate's towers, lanterns at the market arch, the cross, the far benches and the well, a fire basket between the north stalls, a brazier behind the gem bench and two flanking the keeper); a cypress pair flanking each road past its gate; the grey-box shrine cube hidden in game with its collision kept and a Necropolis plinth and tall pillar standing in its place as the town's landmark; the soul-keeper's block swapped for a small Necropolis stone pillar (its Marker, so the click, the walk and the reach are unchanged); the sewer grate's slab in the cave floor's instance under a worn decal with brick piles and a pipe stub; and every `GBT_Wall_*` and `GBT_Tower_*` cube hidden in game, collision kept, under MedCastle castle wall runs (the low south and west walls sunk so the cubes' 200 uu still shows) and square towers at the gatehouses and the three true corners. The gates' slabs and plates stay bare (the pack's gate frame is a house door's, and its arched gate a curved arcade). Everything is NoCollision dressing in `Town/Dress/<Place>` folders; the cubes under it still carry the collision.

A new biome is one more instance with its own two texture sets (CT-2.18, waiting on the biome list). The painted-layer landscape material for the one map calls the same `MF_GroundSet` per layer (CT-2.17). Every number is an eye placeholder; there is no design rule for the ground look.

## Teleport point markers

A teleport point shows where it is and where it goes (CT-2.12). The `ATeleportPoint` actor carries two cosmetic components beside its trigger: a looping Niagara effect on the ground, picked per placed point in the editor (`NS_TeleportZone` from the RPGEnvironmentVFX pack, a ring of fire, is the one used so far), and a floating text label that reads the point's `DestinationTag` unless a `LabelText` override is typed in. The label's height, size, colour and rotation are properties on the point; the rotation defaults to face the ARPG camera (pitch 55, yaw 135 against the camera's -55, -45), so the text reads upright from the play view. Nothing here replicates or ticks: the actor is placed in the map, so every machine has the components, and the effect loops on its own.

The two points placed today are the starter cave's, labelled "Drop down" and "Sewer ladder". A point whose `LabelText` is empty reads its destination tag.

This is a placeholder so testers find the door. What a teleport point looks like in the game (a lit doorway, a signpost, a glowing gate, a banner on entry) is a design decision not taken.

## Wells on a cooldown (E-2.232, 2026-10-14)

Walking into a well always binds it as the checkpoint. The heal is its own rule now: a well fills health, mana and both flasks for a player whose cooldown on that well is over, and then starts that player's cooldown, 120 s (`WellCooldownSeconds` in `DT_CharacterRules`). Each player has a timer of their own on each well, replicated to that player alone and not saved. Inside the cooldown the well does nothing and says "The well refills in N s". A player still standing in the radius when the time is up gets the well's use then, if there is something to fill (since 2026-10-19). A walk into a well with health, mana and both flasks full is no use at all and starts no cooldown; since 2026-10-25 the well then watches that player once a second while they stand in it, and uses itself the moment something needs filling, so a player hurt while standing in a ready well is healed there. A respawn at a well neither heals by the well nor starts its cooldown. Before this a well healed only when it became the bound checkpoint, so coming back hurt to the same well did nothing. `Slime.WellCooldown 0` puts that back. Each machine draws its own player's state: water near the rim when ready, low in the shaft while cooling and rising back, with the time left on a small label above the well.
