# The HUD

*Built in roadmap item E-2.17 (pull request #41). A placeholder layout: no design rule sets what the HUD shows, so this is marked provisional.*

## What you see

Two orbs at the bottom of the screen, red for health and blue for mana, that fill and drain as your numbers change. Between them, a bar of six ability slots labelled 1, 2, 3, 4, LMB and RMB, and above that a thin gold experience bar with your level number at its left (see [Progression](progression.md)). When you die the whole screen dims and shows "You died", the experience lost, and a countdown until you are back. Each slot shows the icon of the move assigned to it, drawn edge to edge under the key label, or the move's name when its soul has no icon for it, or nothing (since E-2.60). While a move is on cooldown a shade covers its icon from the top and shrinks as the cooldown runs, so the icon fills back from the bottom the way Last Epoch draws it, the seconds left sit large in the middle of the slot, and the slot's frame flashes and fades the moment the move is ready again (since E-2.89, so the state reads from the corner of the eye; shade, font, flash colour and timing are config); while you cannot afford it the slot goes dark and says "mana". Over every other character stands a small plate with its name and a health bar (since E-2.70): green for someone your character would not hurt, another player, and red for something it would, a field monster. Your own character has no plate; the orbs are yours. A dead character's plate goes away with it. When a soul lands in your bag, a line at the top of the screen says "Soul acquired: Corpse" for a few seconds (since E-2.77), and under it, until you open the bag for the first time or equip something, a hint: press I, drag the soul onto the Primary slot. Only your own machine shows it, and only for souls that arrive after you joined; a respawn does not repeat it.

## Using it

- **Click a slot** to open a list of the moves your equipped soul knows, each with its icon beside the name, and pick one. Pick the same move on another slot and it moves there.
- **Press the slot's key** to perform the move (see [Abilities](abilities.md)). Only 1, 2, 3 and 4 are bound today, plus the pad's face buttons since E-2.52; the two mouse-button slots are drawn but do nothing.
- **Open the skill tree** with P, or click the round "Skills P" button right of the RMB slot (since E-2.82). The tree used to open on S; S walks backward now. The button reads the key from the config, so a rebind relabels it.
- **Open the options** with O, or click the round "Options O" button right of the Skills button (since E-2.95). Two sliders: **brightness**, which changes the picture the moment you move it, and **screen shake**, from 0 to 100 %, which scales the small camera shake a landed hit gives you; at 0 there is none (the brief freeze on a hit is not shake and stays). "Reset to defaults" puts both back. Your settings belong to your machine: they are written to your own config file when you let go of a slider, press Reset or close the screen, and are there again the next time you start the game. They are not part of your character and the server never sees them. Opening the options closes the bag or the tree.
- **Rebind a key** on the same screen, under "Keys" (since E-2.53): one row per action, the four slot keys, their four pad buttons, move forward, back, left and right, Bag, Skills and Options, each with a button showing the key it uses now. Click the button, it reads "press a key", press the new key on the keyboard or the pad, and it takes at once: the bar's slot label and the two round buttons relabel, and the old key does nothing from then on. Escape while it waits keeps the old key. A key another action already uses is refused and the line under the rows says which. Mouse buttons cannot be bound (the two mouse slots are the bar's own). The close key, Shift for attack-in-place and F1 are fixed. A rebound movement key replaces only the letter (W, S, D or A); the arrow keys keep walking beside it, and the pad stick is untouched. Rebinds are saved with the other options on your machine and are there after a restart; "Reset to defaults" clears them too.
- **Unequip your soul** and every slot clears. Equip a soul and its first move lands in the first empty slot on its own (since E-2.44), so you can fight before touching the picker; the picker still moves it wherever you like.

- **Close any screen** (the bag, the tree, the options, the debug menu) with the X at its top right or with Escape, which closes whichever is on top (since E-2.92). Escape with nothing open does nothing. In Play In Editor the editor keeps Escape for itself, so there use the X or the screen's own key.

A click on a screen's panel stays on the screen (since E-2.92). Before that, a click on any bare part of a panel, or on some of the tree's buttons, went through to the world and your character walked off while you were reading a tooltip; the 2026-09-21 playtest hit it within minutes. A click beside the panel still moves you, as in other ARPGs, so you can keep walking with the bag open.

One thing that was wrong before E-2.82 and is fixed with it: after any screen closed, the tree or the bag, the keyboard was dead until you clicked the game again. With click-to-move nobody noticed; with W A S D it was the first thing anyone hit.

Slot assignments are a menu choice on your machine. They are never sent to the server and never saved, so two players in the same game can arrange their bars differently and nothing needs to agree. Key rebinds are the same kind of thing, except that they are saved.

## How it works, in plain terms

The orbs read the same health and mana numbers the server replicates to you; they never show a value the server has not confirmed. The same goes for the cooldown countdown: the server sends when each move will be ready and the slot compares that with the server's clock. The bar holds a list of six slot definitions loaded from a config file: the label, the key, and nothing else. A move's icon is a texture on its row of the soul asset (`FSoulMove::Icon`), looked up when a slot changes, never per frame; the eight Creepy and Corpse icons were generated in the soul-orb style and set by hand on the soul assets. Adding a slot, renaming one or changing its key is a config edit. A name plate is a widget the pawn itself carries, drawn on the screen above its capsule rather than in the world, so a wall never hides it and distance never shrinks it; it reads the same replicated health as the orbs, and asks your character whether it is hostile to that pawn to pick its colour, which is why a PvP rule would turn other players red without a UI change. The name is the player's name from the session, or for a monster the display name of the soul it wears.

## The debug menu (F1)

*Roadmap item E-2.73. Dev tooling, not a game feature: it exists in editor and Development builds and is compiled out of Shipping the same way the `Slime.*` console commands are.*

Press **F1** and a panel opens at the left edge of the screen; press it again and it closes. It is hidden until then. At the top it shows your character's name, whether this machine is the host or a client, your level, the zone you stand in, health and mana, and whether you are dead or in a safe zone. Below that, a Souls block: a search box, a dropdown of every soul asset in the project (a new soul shows up by itself) and Grant, Equip primary and Wear buttons for the one picked. Then buttons: set a level or add experience; drop health or mana to zero or fill them; grant a gem of each colour; spawn a number of Corpse or Creepy monsters in front of you; switch the movement-rooting and attack-feel toggles; two camera presets; and buttons that print attributes, gems and the server rules to the Output Log. A **target pawn** dropdown lists every character in the world so a button can act on another player's character instead of your own. A text box at the bottom runs any console command you type, and the last line the command logged is echoed under it.

Every button runs one existing console command on your machine, exactly as if you had typed it. That means the same rule applies as at the console: things that change the game (granting, levelling, spawning, setting health) only work on the host, because the server owns those outcomes; on a joiner's machine the button logs "run on the host" and nothing happens. The feel toggles and the camera are local and work anywhere. Nothing new is sent to the server and nothing is saved.

## What is waiting on design

- **Everything about layout.** Slot count, keys, what an orb should look like. The team has asked for a design item covering the HUD and hotbar; until then this is a placeholder shell, registered as such.
- **Orb art.** The fill is a flat disc rising inside a clipped box, drawn in code; no glow, no easing, no texture.

## Minimap (E-2.114 MVP, E-2.117 second pass)

A square in the top-right corner shows the ground around you from above with your dot in the middle. Since E-2.117 the map turns with the camera, so up on the map is up on the screen, the way the Diablo and Path of Exile overlays sit: the ARPG camera looks along a diagonal (yaw -45), and on the first, north-up version a walk up the screen read as a slide to the right. Set `bMinimapRotateWithCamera=False` to get north up (-Y) back. Every region of the map (the `ARegionZone` boxes of E-2.115) is drawn under the fog as a faint tint and an outline in a colour picked by its name (`MinimapRegionColours`: forest green, town stone grey, beach sand, fields gold, jungle dark green, pass grey-white; anything else `MinimapRegionDefaultColour`), so once its ground is revealed you can see where a region ends and the next begins; the town's box stands in for its wall ring. Everything you have not walked near is under a dark fog; walking lifts it in a disc around you, and a teleport drops you into a dark patch that clears as you move. Checkpoint wells appear as white dots once their ground is revealed; other players are dots in their own colours. Monsters are never shown: it is a wayfinding tool, not a radar. The fog is remembered for the session only; whether it is kept per character is a design question still open. The ground picture is the map's own World Partition minimap, which a human builds in the editor (`Build > Build World Partition Minimap`); until then the square shows a flat green. Sizes, radii and colours are the `Minimap*` rows of `Config/DefaultGame.ini` (`UHudLayoutSettings`); `bMinimap=False` removes it. Code: `Source/SandboxARPG/UI/MinimapWidget.*` (`FMinimapFog`, the fog grid; `UMinimapWidget`, painted in `NativePaint`, the fog lifted by a timer from a buffer allocated once), built by `AMonsterHUD::BeginPlay`.

## Region name toast (E-2.115)

The toast card at the top of the screen, which the soul pickup already used, now also shows the name of the region the local pawn just entered (`AMonsterHUD::ShowRegionName`, `UPlayerHud::ShowToast`), for `RegionToastSeconds` (3 s, `UHudLayoutSettings`). Both lines share the one card; the newer replaces the older.

## Arena screen (E-2.116)

An "Arena" disc after Options on the bar opens a small panel: a status line (not queued, in the queue, matched) read from your pawn's replicated arena state, and one button that queues you for a duel or takes you out of the queue. The click is an intent sent to the server; the server's arena queue (`UArenaQueueSubsystem`) matches the first two in line and teleports them to the two spots inside the ring outside the town's south gate. Whether your hits land there is the server's PvP rule, as anywhere outside a safe zone; the arena's own rules are D-2.14, undecided.
