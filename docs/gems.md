# Gems

*Built in roadmap item E-3.2. Design rules: GDD 3.1 to 3.3, equipment is a few slots carrying rolled stat lines. Provisional on the slot memo (D-3.2), which makes gems the equipment layer.*

## The idea

Your equipment is gems. There are no armour or weapon slots and no separate accessory that a gem plugs into: the gem is the item, and it carries the stat lines. You have nine slots, two red, two green, two blue, two that take any colour, and one for special gems. A red gem goes in a red slot or an any slot; a special gem goes only in the special slot. Colour says what the gem carries: red gems hold offense lines, blue gems control and utility, green gems defense, one colour per category of the [affix table](affix-table.md). A gem that breaks that rule is refused when it is created. That mapping is Jon's reading of the design board, written into the slot memo and marked provisional until Duilio confirms it; what the special slot holds is still open.

A gem has a colour, a tier, a rarity name, and a list of lines. The tier runs from I to X and names the gem's stone: a red gem is Red Jasper at tier I, Pyrope Garnet at IV and Ruby at X; blue runs Sodalite to Sapphire, green Aventurine to Emerald, iridescent Common Opal to Black Opal, special Quartz to Diamond. Ten tiers, the stone names and which stones belong to the iridescent and special colours are Jon's placeholders, marked provisional until Duilio records them (E-3.7). Today the tier changes only what the gem looks like and is called; nothing about its lines depends on it yet, and every gem a monster drops is tier I. Each line names one of the 107 entries in the [affix table](affix-table.md) and the value it rolled. Since E-3.3 a gem can be rolled. The roll takes the gem's colour, a rarity and the gem's tier. Rarity is a cap on how many lines, not the count (since E-5.3, provisional on the D-5.4 memo): a white gem holds up to one, a blue up to two, a yellow up to three (the rows R1, R2 and R3 of the data, which the player reads as Normal, Magic and Rare since 2026-11-01), and a drop rolls under its cap, one line fewer than the cap about forty times in a hundred, so a blue can arrive with one line and room for another. Every line is a prefix or a suffix (the `Slot` column of the [affix table](affix-table.md)), a gem holds at most two of each kind, and a three-line gem is two prefixes and a suffix or the other way round on a coin; the tooltip writes a P or an S after each line's tier. For each line the server picks a line family of the colour's category that the gem does not already carry, of a kind that still has room, then one of that family's ten tiers, never above the gem's own tier and leaning toward it, then a number inside that tier's band. So a Ruby X can carry a tier 10 line worth 174 to 227 health-sized points where a Red Jasper I carries 5 to 10, and an R1 gem of a high tier can beat an R3 gem of a low tier on its one line. The same seed always gives the same gem. Every number, the ten tiers and the weights are placeholders for Duilio's balancing pass.

## What happens when you equip one

The server moves the gem from your bag into the slot and applies every line to your stats. Each line's table row says which stat it targets and how:

- **Flat** adds points. Fifty flat maximum health is fifty more.
- **Increased** adds a percentage. Every increased line on the same stat is summed first, then applied once, so two lines of ten percent are twenty percent, not twenty-one.
- **More** multiplies on its own. Two lines of ten percent more are twenty-one percent.

Unequip and the lines come off. Modifiers sit on top of your base stats, so [level and soul growth](progression.md) keep working underneath: a level-up changes the base, the gem's percentage still applies to the new number.

Lines whose target stat does not exist yet are logged and skipped, never applied. Since E-3.9 the offense lines with a step in the damage function act (flat, increased and more damage, typed or not, critical chance and damage, penetration; the Accuracy line was removed in E-3.13); ailment, mark, enemy-state, skill-type, minion, conversion, extra-as and double-damage lines still wait for their mechanics. Nothing is persisted; contract C-4 carries gems later.

## What you see

Your gems sit in the bag on the [inventory screen](inventory-shell.md) as their stones, each with its tier numeral in the corner of the cell and a frame in its rarity's colour (since E-3.8: light grey for a one-line gem, blue for two lines, gold for three; a gem granted by a dev command with no rarity has a dark grey frame); hover for the stone's name in its rarity's colour, a line naming the rarity and colour ("Rare Red gem") and the lines, and each line reads as it acts, in the word guide's form since E-3.10: "+14 maximum health" for points, "+12% Physical damage" for an increased line, "8% more damage" for a more line, "4% chance to Burn" for a chance, followed by the line's tier and the band it could have rolled in, "T2 (+11 to +18)", so you can tell a high roll from a low one. A slotted gem's frame is its rarity too. A slotted gem and a gem being dragged show the same stone. A gem whose colour and tier have no stone shows the plain colour icon instead. Drag one onto a slot of its colour to equip it (an iridescent gem, a fifth colour with no lines decided yet, goes only in the two iridescent slots, and the other four colours never do, since E-3.6); drag it back to the bag to take it off. The health orb moves when the lines land. Since E-3.5.

## Drops

Since E-2.32 a field monster can drop a gem when it dies: half the time at the host's default drop rate, a pickup at the corpse that anyone can walk over. Since E-3.8 the pickup shows the gem's own stone, the same icon as in the bag, on a soft glow in its rarity's colour with no frame or box behind it (since E-2.146; E-3.8 drew a frame), hung a little above the ground and always the same size on screen whatever the camera distance, so a ruby glowing gold on the ground is told from a jasper glowing grey before anyone walks to it; a gem whose stone has no icon falls back to the old tinted sphere. The first player to touch it gets it in their bag; it vanishes for everyone, and an untaken one fades after two minutes. Each drop is one colour and one fixed line, from a list of five: a red one with flat damage (which does nothing yet), blue ones with maximum mana or mana regeneration, green ones with health or defense. With `Slime.AffixRoll` on (the default since E-3.3) a drop is rolled instead: a colour, a rarity by weight, and its lines from the [affix table](affix-table.md); `Slime.AffixRoll 0` brings the five fixed lines back as a developer fallback. Since E-3.4 (provisional on the 2026-09-23 zone-level research note) the monster decides the rest: its level sets the gem's tier (tier I up to level 5, II up to 10, and so on), an elite adds one tier and a boss two; a gem's lines come from its own tier and at most two below, so a jungle gem never carries a tier I line; a normal monster drops one gem half the time, an elite always drops one and has a second chance, a boss drops three; an elite's gems lean rare (a quarter one-line, half two-line, a quarter three-line against 60 / 30 / 10 for a normal) and a boss never drops a one-line gem. Several gems fan out around the corpse. The host's Drop rates rule scales the chances, never the guaranteed count.

**Drop labels (E-2.185, 2026-10-06).** With the theme on, a gem on the ground wears a dark label under its stone with the stone's name in its rarity's colour ("Carnelian II"), as Diablo names its drops; a placed soul pickup wears "<Soul> soul" in gold. Provisional (register row `ui look`); the label is part of `UGemPickupWidget`, which `ASoulPickup` now carries too, stone-less.

**Dropping a gem by hand (E-2.195, 2026-10-07).** Drag a gem out of the bag or out of a socket and let go over the game world, not over the inventory panel, a slot or the bottom console, and it lands on the ground one step in front of you as an ordinary pickup. Anyone who walks onto it takes it, you included; it lands just out of your own reach so it is not picked straight back up. It lies for 180 seconds and is then destroyed (a monster's drop lies 120). It works in a safe zone; a dead player drops nothing; a socketed gem stops giving its lines the moment it is dropped. Souls cannot be dropped: a soul's drag let go over the world does nothing, as before. The server checks that the gem is really yours before it drops it. `Slime.DropGem <bag index>` does the same without a mouse. Provisional on Jon's words (register row `gem drop by drag`), behind `bDragToDropGems`; the numbers are `PlayerDropLifetimeSeconds` and `PlayerDropClearance` in `UGemDropSettings`.

A line that adds a fraction reads as a percent on the gem: movement speed, the four resistances and crowd-control resistance show "+3%" where they showed "+0.03". The movement speed line rolls 2 to 3 % at tier I up to 20 to 21 % at tier X since E-2.151; it used to roll whole points, and a +4 made the pawn walk five times as fast.

## For testers

Host-only commands, except the equip pair which a client may run and the server then decides:

- `Slime.GrantGem Green FlatHealth_T01=50 IncreasedHealth_T01=0.1` puts a green gem with two defense lines in your bag. A number after the colour is the tier, 1 to 10, tier 1 without one: `Slime.GrantGem Red 10` is a Ruby. Add a pawn name before the lines to give it to someone else. Line ids are `DT_Affixes` row names, a line and its tier; a red gem with a health line is refused.
- `Slime.RollGem Green R3 42 10` rolls a green three-line gem of tier X with seed 42 and puts it in your bag; the first number is the seed (random without one), the second the tier (1 without one). Run it twice and the two gems match line for line.
- `Slime.EquipGem 0 0` equips bag gem 0 into slot 0. A colour the slot refuses is logged and rejected.
- `Slime.UnequipGem 0` sends it back to the bag.
- `Slime.ShowGems` prints every pawn's bag and slots as this machine sees them; `Slime.ShowAttributes` shows the result.

## A full bag (E-2.215)

The bag has forty cells for gems; the souls you carry sit above them, one cell a soul, and take nothing from them (E-2.251, GDD 2.8). When every gem cell is taken, a gem you walk onto stays on the ground and a line tells you the bag is full; a gem cannot be taken out of a socket either until a cell is free. Before this a gem picked up with a full bag was kept but never shown, which looked as if it had been destroyed. Souls are never turned away. Forty is a placeholder: later items may take more than one cell. Since 2026-10-19 a gem you pick up still goes in when the bag is full if an empty socket of its colour waits for it and your option to auto-equip gems is on: it goes straight into that socket.

For engineers: `UGemEquipmentComponent::GrantGem` and `UnequipGem` ask `HasBagRoom` on the authority (`EvaluateBagHasRoom` over `GetBagCapacity`, the grid's rows times columns, counting the gems alone); a refusal calls the owner's `ClientBagFull`, throttled to one in two seconds. `RestoreGem` is never refused. The inventory grows its cell pools a row at a time when more is held than the grid has cells.

## Picking a gem up (E-2.213)

A gem on the ground is picked up with a left click. Walking over it does nothing. Gems dropped on one spot stand apart, each on a spiral round the first (since 2026-10-19). Click the stone or its name label and your character walks to it and takes it when it gets there; click somewhere else, or move, and it stops. One click takes one gem. If a monster stands on the gem, a click on the monster's body attacks it as always, and a click on the gem's name label always means the gem. If the bag is full the gem stays where it is. This holds for gems you dropped yourself and for gems dropped on death; souls on the ground are still taken by walking over them. A gem hidden behind your own body cannot be clicked until you step off it. Since 2026-10-13 a Pick up key does the same without the cursor: F, or the right shoulder on a pad (both can be rebound on the options page), takes the gem nearest you within 600 uu, walking to it first; one press, one gem.

For engineers: `AMonsterCharacter::PressLeftButton` asks `FindGemUnderCursor` (each drop's drawn stone and label, `UGemPickupWidget::GetViewportRects`) and enters the `PickUp` mode, which path-follows to the gem and sends `UGemEquipmentComponent::RequestPickUpGem` within `PickupReach` of the capsule's edge. The authority's `PickUpGem` re-checks through `EvaluatePickUpRefusal` (flag, alive, pickup still there, reach plus `PickupReachSlack`) and ends in `AGemPickup::TryGiveTo`. `UGemDropSettings::bClickToPickUpGems` off restores the walk-over. Dev: `Slime.PickUpGem`, `Slime.GemPoint [label|stone] [click]`.

## The crafting record (E-5.4)

Every gem now carries what the bench and alchemy will read: a polish step count on each line (0 to 4, each step +5 % of the line's value, applied when the gem is equipped and rounded to the line's band), and two states, Cut (sealed: nothing but corruption and salvage touches it again) and Corrupted (nothing but salvage). The tooltip shows each of them: `+12 damage (T3, P) (+10 %)`, `Ruby X · Cut`. There is no budget of alterations on a gem: the shards are the budget (Jon, 2026-10-17).

The character holds counts for crafting on a component of its own: white, blue and yellow shards today, reagents and soul essence later. The counts replicate to their owner alone and are saved with the character beside the gems. Every craft is one request to the server, which re-reads the gem, the counts and the budget before it moves anything, and tells the player why when it refuses. No operation exists yet: salvage and the step-up are E-5.5, the bench's Polish, Grind and Cut are E-5.6.

A record saved before this change loads with no counts and every gem at its full budget. Assumed on the D-5.4 memo (register row `gem crafting`); the record change is reviewed by Jeff before it merges.

## Salvage and shards (E-5.5)

Any gem can be broken into shards of its rarity: white shards from a white gem, blue from a blue, yellow from a yellow. A gem of tier I or II gives one shard, III to V two, VI to VIII three, IX and X four. A socketed gem can be salvaged in place; its lines come off the character as it goes. Three shards of one kind become one of the next kind up (white to blue, blue to yellow), never the other way. Shards are counts on the character, saved with it, never items in the bag and never dropped on death.

A script beside the affix generator (`scripts/check_crafting_economy.py`) proves the placeholder numbers hold no loop that makes shards from shards. Assumed on the D-5.4 memo (register row `gem crafting`).

## The gem bench (E-5.6)

A workbench stands in the town square by the well (a grey block until its real body lands). Click it or press the pick-up key beside it and two panels open on the right of the screen: your inventory, and the bench beside it (E-5.13, Jon's layout of 2026-10-17). The bench has one socket in its middle for the gem you are working on: Shift + click a gem in your bag or a socket, or drag it onto the socket, and it is on the bench (its cell in the bag goes dim with a gold line); click the socket to take it off. Under the socket: the gem's name, its affixes (click one to choose it), the work buttons with their prices, greyed with the reason when they cannot act, the hammer and the salvage-all beside it, and your shard counts as three pictures (white, blue, yellow) with their numbers. Walk away, press Escape or close either panel and both close.

- **Polish** raises one chosen affix by 5 % of its rolled value, four times at most (+20 %), for 1, 2, 3 then 4 white shards. The button says what the step gives before you pay ("Polish: +41 to +43 (1 white shard)"); the affix shows the number you get, with the polish after it: a +41 polished once reads "+43 ... (polished +5%)", four times "+49 ... (polished +20%)"; the roll underneath is kept, so a tier upgrade keeps the polish. A step that would leave the number as it is (5 % of a small whole-point value rounds away: a +7 reads +7 after one step) is refused and costs nothing; the button reads "Polish: +7 is too small to improve" and the notice "Polishing would not change that affix."
- **Raise tier** takes one chosen affix up a tier with an Expanding Lattice; see the bench's items below. The rule under it is Grind's: the affix keeps its place in the new tier's band, no reroll. Grind itself has no button; `Slime.BenchCraft grind` still runs it for shards.
- **Cut** is not on the bench (since 2026-11-02). Its rule is kept for the dev command `Slime.Cut`: a Rare gem is filled to four affixes, two prefixes and two suffixes, for yellow shards, then sealed, so nothing at the bench touches it again but salvage.
- **Salvage** is the hammer. Click it and the cursor becomes a hammer; every gem you then click in your bag or sockets is broken into shards of its rarity, one click each, as many as you like. A Rare, cut or corrupted gem asks twice: the first click turns its cell red for three seconds, the second breaks it. The gem on the bench cannot be hammered until you take it off. A right click, Escape, or the hammer button again puts the hammer down. What each button does is its tooltip: there are no words beside the pictures.
- **Salvage all** is the heap of shards right of the hammer, captioned so: every gem in your bag breaks at once, into the shards each would give; the gem on the bench and your socketed gems are left alone. A click opens a card with Yes and No that names what would break and what you would get; Yes does it, and the notice line then reads, for example, "5 gems salvaged: 3 white, 2 blue, 1 yellow shards".
- The two **step-ups** read "3 [white] to 1 [blue]" and "3 [blue] to 1 [yellow]", the shard pictures in the line: three of the lower kind for one of the higher, never the other way. They touch no gem; the counts at the foot are where you see it.

Every click is a request the server checks: that you stand at the bench, hold the gem, have the shards, and that the gem is open to the operation. A refusal names its reason on the notice line as a whole sentence (E-5.15, 2026-10-30). Affix tags read in words: "(tier 3 prefix)" where they read "(T3, P)". Assumed on the D-5.4 memo (register row `gem crafting`).

## The bench's items: the Etching Needle and the Expanding Lattice (E-5.16 to E-5.21, 2026-11-01)

Two things drop beside gems now, on Jon's rulings of 2026-10-31 (D-5.4 section 14). An **Etching Needle** adds one affix to a gem at the bench; an **Expanding Lattice** raises one affix a tier. A monster drops a needle about one kill in fifty and a lattice one in thirty-three (an Elite one in seven and one in five; a Boss drops one of each, always), scaled by the host's Drop rates like a gem's chance. They lie on the ground with their picture (the lattice's is a green cube, `T_Icon_Item_ExpandingLattice`) and name ("Etching Needle", "Expanding Lattice x3") and are taken as a gem is, by a click or the pick-up key. In the bag each kind is one cell with its count in the corner, counted against the forty; a stack holds fifty at most, and a pickup past that is refused with the Inventory full line. Drag the cell onto the ground and the whole stack lies there for three minutes for anyone to take. They are never salvaged or stepped up: an item only ever goes into a gem. The stacks are saved with the character beside the shards.

At the bench, with a gem on it:

- **Add affix** costs one Etching Needle and nothing else. The gem's count goes from n to n + 1, up to nine, five prefixes and five suffixes at most. The chance is by the gem's count before the add, 100 / 100 / 90 / 75 / 60 / 45 / 30 / 20 % for the 2nd to the 9th affix, times a factor for the gem's tier: whole up to tier V, then 95, 90, 85, 80 and 75 % at tiers VI to X (a tier X gem's 4th affix is 90 % of 75 %, shown as 67 %). The button says it before you click: "Add affix: a random prefix or suffix · 67% chance · 1 Etching Needle". The needle is spent either way; on a failure the gem is unchanged. The new affix is a random family of the gem's colour, in a random kind with room, at a tier up to two under the gem's; you never choose it. A cut gem takes no new affix.
- **Raise tier** costs one Expanding Lattice and nothing else: the chosen affix goes one tier up, as far as tier X whatever the gem's own tier, its value as high in the new range as it was in the old. The chance is by the tier it goes onto: sure up to tier IV, then 90, 80, 70, 60, 50 and 40 % onto tiers V to X. The button shows it ("Raise tier: +18% minion damage to tier 6 · 80% chance · 1 Expanding Lattice"). The lattice is spent either way; on a failure the affix stays at its tier. A cut gem may still be raised. The host's `CraftingForgiveness` rule scales the fail share of both items.
- **Polish**, the hammer and the step-ups are as before. Polish is the one bench action that spends shards, so blue and yellow shards have no use but the step-up for now.
- **The outcome card** (E-5.23): after a needle or a lattice a card over the bench panel says AFFIX ADDED or TIER RAISED in gold, with the new affix (before and after for a raise), or THE NEEDLE FAILED or THE LATTICE FAILED in red, with the gem or affix unchanged. OK, the next bench action or the gem leaving the bench closes it; a refusal still goes to the notice line. The card covers the work buttons while it stands, so one click cannot be spent twice.
- **The bench itself** wears the label "Gemcrafting Bench" over it, drawn always, and while the cursor is on it a gold rim (the overlay material `M_BenchHoverRim`) lights its meshes and its label. The needle and lattice cells in the bag look like a gem's cell.

A gem's rarity word and colour now follow its affix count: one affix white, two or three blue, four or more yellow, never under what it dropped as. The drop still rolls its count by its rarity; the bench carries a gem up the bands. Salvage reads the rarity it dropped with. Every number is a placeholder of the economy pass. ## Whole numbers only (E-2.244, 2026-10-23)

Every gem affix rolls, polishes, grinds and reads as a whole number (a point line) or a whole percent (a fraction line, a multiple of 0.01). One rule, `FCraftingRules::ValueIsPercent`, decides which for the roll, the polish and grind rounding and the text; `gen_affix_tiers.py` refuses a band step other than 1 or 0.01. The two regeneration lines now step by whole points (1 at tier I, 18 to 19 at tier X). A gem rolled before the rule keeps its stored value and shows and applies it rounded.
