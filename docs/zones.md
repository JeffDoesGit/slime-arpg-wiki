# Zones: the town gate, the safe zone and the one map

*Built in roadmap items E-2.41 (the gate), E-2.9b (the safe zone), CT-2.16 and E-2.76a to E-2.76d (the one open map and its teleport points). The gate is provisional on the human-soul memo (D-2.7); the safe zone is the settled rule GDD 6.1; one open map per server is the settled rule GDD 5.7.*

## What you see

Outside the town stands a gate that will not let you through until you wear the soul lying on the ground nearby (the human soul, in the Worn slot of the inventory; a flag on the gate can relax this to owning it). Take it, wear it, touch the gate, and the door goes away for everyone on the server for the rest of the session.

Inside the town, or any place marked as safe, nothing can hurt you and you can hurt nothing. Press an ability key and nothing happens; a monster's swing does not land; a projectile that reaches you pops harmlessly. Monsters outside stop caring about you the moment you step in, and pick you up again when you step out. If you wear a soul (the human), you show its body while you are inside and your primary's body again once you step out (see [Souls](soul-model.md)).

The world is one map. You walk from the town's east arch across a strip of grass and under Zone1's arch, and nothing loads, nothing teleports, nothing is written to the log. The forest is the one place with a door: its gate is a teleport point, one way, into the town's south-west corner. Step on it and you stand in the town a moment later. If you joined someone else's game you may stand still for a moment first, with a card that says "Travelling", until your own machine has the ground around you; then you walk.

## How it works, in plain words

**The gate** is one placed actor: a wall of collision every pawn hits, an invisible trigger in front of it, and a door mesh. When a player touches the trigger the server checks whether that player's character owns the gate's soul, in either slot or in the bag. The first owner opens it: the server flips one flag, the flag replicates, and every machine drops the wall and hides the door. There is no key item and no per-player state; an open gate is open.

**The safe zone** is a placed box. The server marks every pawn inside it, player or monster, and clears the mark when it leaves; a pawn standing in two overlapping zones stays marked until it has left both. The mark is one replicated flag on the pawn, and every combat rule reads that flag rather than asking the box:

- a move pressed while marked is refused on the server before anything plays, and the owning client refuses it locally too so no request goes out;
- a hit whose target or attacker is marked is not applied, with no flinch and no miss roll;
- a projectile that reaches a marked pawn pops without a hit;
- a monster never picks a marked pawn as its target and drops one that becomes marked.

Death cannot happen inside, so the death penalty never applies there; "no loss" follows from "no combat".

**The one map** is `Content/Levels/Lvl_Slice_WP`, a World Partition map (GDD 5.7): the forest, the town and Zone1 laid out on one landscape as the design board draws them, the town at the centre, the forest to the south-west with its gate into the town's south-west corner, Zone1 to the east, joined to the town by open ground. One light rig, one navmesh, one safe zone over the town. The engine streams the ground around each player by itself, in cells; the server keeps the whole map resident (server streaming is off, the engine's default), so a player can be moved anywhere on it at any time and the ground is already there for the server. The map was built new rather than converted, because the engine's convert commandlet stops on a landscape that lives in a sublevel and the old landscape held nothing worth keeping (`docs/plans/level-changes-2026-09-22.md`). It is the map the game opens: `GameDefaultMap` and `EditorStartupMap` in `Config/DefaultEngine.ini` name it and the packaging command in `docs/playtest.md` cooks it alone (the cook writes the streaming cells itself). The town keeps its cobble cube with its top 2 cm above the landscape until painted layers exist (CT-2.17); Zone1's grass cube is gone, the landscape is its floor.

## Moving around the map

Most of the map you walk. A **teleport point** (`ATeleportPoint`) is a box a player walks into; it names a **teleport destination** (`ATeleportDestination`) by a tag, and destinations are never streamed out, so the server can always find them. On the map today there is one point, the forest's gate, and its destination is the one tagged `FromForest` on the cobble inside the town's south-west corner. Walk into the point and the server moves you to the destination on its next poll, standing still and facing the way the destination faces. A walk you had clicked ends the moment you touch the point, so you cannot walk on into the same point again (E-2.64). If someone is already standing on the destination, you land on a free spot a step or two around it instead; only when every spot is blocked are you set down on the destination itself and the characters shuffle apart, so a crowd at a door never leaves anyone behind (E-2.67).

**The one map exists (CT-2.16).** `Content/Levels/Lvl_Slice_WP` is that World Partition map: the forest, the town and Zone1 on one landscape laid out as the design board draws them (the town at the centre, the forest to the south-west with its one-way gate into the town's south-west corner, Zone1 to the east; the west, south and north walls are kept for the next fields), one light rig, the same doors, gate, pickup, spawners and safe zone as the three zone maps, copied across with their per-actor edits. It was built new rather than converted, because the engine's convert commandlet stops on a landscape that lives in a sublevel and the old landscape held nothing worth keeping (`docs/plans/level-changes-2026-09-22.md`). Since E-2.76b it is the map the game opens: `GameDefaultMap` and `EditorStartupMap` in `Config/DefaultEngine.ini` name it, the packaging command in `docs/playtest.md` cooks it alone (the cook writes the streaming cells itself), and `Lvl_Slice` with its three zone maps stays in the project uncooked until the content PR that deletes them. On it the town and Zone1 are joined by open ground with no door between them: you walk out of the town's east arch, across a strip of grass, under Zone1's arch, and nothing loads, teleports or is logged. The forest keeps its one door, one-way into the town's south-west corner: since E-2.76c it is a teleport point (`ATeleportPoint`), and the CT-2.12 marker therefore shows on this map only at the forest gate. The town keeps its cobble cube with its top 2 cm above the landscape until painted layers exist (CT-2.17); Zone1's grass cube is gone, the landscape is its floor.

If you joined someone else's game, your machine streams the cells around your new position only after the server has moved you there. Until then you would be standing on ground your machine does not have yet, so you are held: the server turns your movement off until your machine reports the cell under your feet, and your own machine holds itself from the moment the travel starts until it has landed and the engine says your cells have streamed, or until ten seconds have passed, whichever comes first (E-2.57, `ArrivalTimeoutSeconds`). The card that says "Travelling" is that hold: it shows while you are travelling and goes away when your machine lets you walk. Everyone else sees you go. Your bag, souls, gems, level and experience travel with you because they live on your character.

The place you last arrived at is your checkpoint. If you die and pick the checkpoint, you come back at that destination; if you have never taken a teleport, you come back at the level's PlayerStart. If you pick the town, you come back at the town's destination, `FromForest` (E-2.46, `TownDestination` in `Config/DefaultGame.ini`). What a checkpoint is in the game (a well, a crystal, the gazebo) and whether it heals you is still design's (D-2.12); which places get teleport points is design's too (D-8.6 memo §8.6).

**What is gone.** Everything the old door-streamed zones did is gone with them: there are no zone sublevels, no zone name on your character, no "Entering <zone>" card, no zone loaded before the first player spawns, no zone kept loaded because players join into it, no zone unloaded half a minute after its last player leaves, no per-player loading and dropping of zones, and no distinction between the host's view and a joiner's beyond the arrival hold above. A late join cannot fall into an unloaded starting zone any more, because there is no zone to unload: the engine never streams a PlayerStart out, and the server holds the whole map. The old maps (`Lvl_Slice`, `StartingForest`, `StartingTown`, `Zone1`) stay in the project uncooked until the content change that deletes them; nothing plays them, and the code that streamed them no longer exists.

## Settled

- GDD 6.1: cities are safe zones, no combat, no loss. The zone is the rule's whole implementation for now.
- GDD 5.7: one open world map per server, streamed by cell around every player. `Lvl_Slice_WP` is that map; teleport points are for one-way doors and distant places, never for a seam between regions.
- The server validation list (DS-0.3 §3 row 12): every damage application is checked against the safe-zone rule on the authority.

## Ground materials

Every floor in the slice wears one of two instances of a game-owned master material, `M_GroundTile` (`Content/Art/Materials/Ground/`). The master tiles in world space, through the pack's `MF_WorldCoords-XY`, so the landscape and a scaled floor cube show the same texture size without any UV work. It holds two texture sets, each one call of the material function `MF_GroundSet` (base colour, normal, an optional AORM map), and mixes them by a world-space noise. The parameters are `TileSize`, `TileScaleB` (set B tiles at a multiple of set A), `BlendThreshold`, `BlendContrast`, `BlendStrength`, `NoiseScale`, `Roughness` and `Tint`.

- `MI_Ground_Grass`: grass over dirt, dirt only in the noise peaks (threshold 0.68, contrast 6). On the landscape of `Lvl_Slice_WP`, which is the forest's and Zone1's floor.
- `MI_Ground_Cobble`: the same stone in both sets, set B at 1.6 times the size, blended softly, so no two tiles line up. On the town's floor cube.

A new biome is one more instance with its own two texture sets (CT-2.18, waiting on the biome list). The painted-layer landscape material for the one map calls the same `MF_GroundSet` per layer (CT-2.17). Every number is an eye placeholder; there is no design rule for the ground look.

For testers: a floor that shows the grey grid material means an instance failed to compile; the log line starts `Failed to compile Material Instance with Base M_GroundTile`. A texture with sRGB on cannot feed the AORM slot (`Sampler type is Linear Color, should be Color`).

## Teleport point markers

A teleport point shows where it is and where it goes (CT-2.12). The `ATeleportPoint` actor carries two cosmetic components beside its trigger: a looping Niagara effect on the ground, picked per placed point in the editor (the forest gate uses `NS_TeleportZone` from the RPGEnvironmentVFX pack, a ring of fire), and a floating text label that reads the point's `DestinationTag` unless a `LabelText` override is typed in. The label's height, size, colour and rotation are properties on the point; the rotation defaults to face the ARPG camera (pitch 55, yaw 135 against the camera's -55, -45), so the text reads upright from the play view. Nothing here replicates or ticks: the actor is placed in the map, so every machine has the components, and the effect loops on its own.

The one point on the map today is the forest gate into the town; in the gate the walls hide most of the ring from the camera and the label does the work. The label used to read the destination zone's name; a point now names only a destination tag, so a placed point whose `LabelText` is empty reads that tag (`FromForest`) until a human types a label under lock.

This is a placeholder so testers find the door. What a teleport point looks like in the game (a lit doorway, a signpost, a glowing gate, a banner on entry) is a design decision not taken.

## For testers

- Place a `SafeZoneVolume` and size its Extent to cover the area. It never blocks movement.
- Inside it, an ability press logs `USoulComponent: <pawn> pressed '<move>' in a safe zone; refused` on the server.
- A monster losing you logs `<monster>: target lost`; a hit refused logs `Hit: <attacker> on <target> not applied (safe zone)`.
- `Slime.SetAttribute Health 0 <pawn>` still kills inside: it is a dev command, not damage.
- The gate logs `SoulGate <name>: opened by <pawn> (wears <soul>)` and `... does not wear ...; stays shut` (or owns / own with `bRequireWorn` off).
- A teleport logs on the host `Teleport: <pawn> leaving for <tag>` and `Teleport: <pawn> arrived at <destination> (destination)` (or `ring N spot M`, or `destination, forced`). A joiner's arrival adds `Teleport: holding <pawn> until its client shows the cell of <ground>` and `Teleport: released <pawn> after N s; its client shows the ground at <tag>`, or `not held (its client already shows the ground's cell)`; the joiner's own log shows `Teleport: client holds <pawn> until its destination is shown here` and `Teleport: client released <pawn> after N s (destination shown here)`. A release that says `timeout` means the ten seconds passed first. A tag the map does not hold logs `asked for destination '<tag>', which this map does not hold; ignored`.
- `Slime.Teleport <DestinationTag> [PawnName]` on the host moves a pawn to a destination through the same path as a point (`Slime.Teleport FromForest`, `Slime.Teleport FromTown MonsterCharacter_1`). `Slime.Zone <IgnoredZoneName> <DestinationTag> [PawnName]` is the old spelling and still works; its first argument is ignored. Dev builds only.
- A point with no ring has no Niagara system set on its `Marker` component; a point with no label has an empty `DestinationTag` and no `LabelText`. Both are set on the placed actor, under lock.
- The debug menu's header (F1) reads `travelling` while the pawn is between a point and its landing, or held on arrival.

## For engineers

`Zones/TeleportSubsystem.*`: `RequestTeleport(Pawn, DestinationTag)` on the authority adds a pending teleport and calls the pawn's `BeginTravel`; `PollPending` lands every pending teleport on the next poll through `Arrive` (`TeleportTo` at the destination, the E-2.67 ring search around it, `SetLastDestination`, `EndTravel`) and, for a remote player, holds the pawn (`SetHeldForArrival`) until `UNetConnection::ClientHasInitializedLevel` reports the level of the actor the Floor channel finds under the arrival spot (`FindGroundUnder`), the engine's own visibility report, no RPC of ours (contract C-1), or until `ArrivalTimeoutSeconds` passes; `FindDestination` walks a `TActorIterator<ATeleportDestination>` by tag; `IsShownHere(Viewer)` is `UWorldPartitionSubsystem::IsStreamingCompleted(Viewer)` on a partitioned map and true on any other. It assumes server streaming stays off (`wp.Runtime.EnableServerStreaming=0`, the 5.8 default). `Zones/TeleportActors.*` (`ATeleportPoint`, `ATeleportDestination`; the class and property redirects in `Config/DefaultEngine.ini` load the actors placed as `ZoneExit` and `ZoneEntrance`), `Zones/TeleportConsoleCommands.cpp` (`Slime.Teleport`, `Slime.Zone`). On the pawn: `TravelSerial` (replicated push-model, odd while travelling; `BeginTravel` and `EndTravel` bump it on the authority), `OnRep_TravelSerial` (the owning client stops its path and holds itself, `bHeldByClient`, `PollHeld` on a 0.1 s timer), `bHeldForArrival` (the authority's hold), `IsTravelling` (what the HUD card and the debug header read), `LastDestinationTag` (the respawn's checkpoint; `TownDestination` on the game mode is the town's). `ApplyMovementHold` is still the one place that picks the movement mode from death, the arrival hold and the move hold. Gone: `UZoneSubsystem`, `AZoneExit`, `AZoneEntrance`, the zone names on the pawn, the client RPC, `InitialZone`, `TownZone`, the unload timers and the initial-zone flush in `InitGame`. `Source/SandboxARPG/Zones/SoulGate.*` (the gate) and `Zones/SafeZoneVolume.*` (the zone) are unchanged. The safe-zone flag lives on `Player/MonsterCharacter` (`IsInSafeZone`, `SetInSafeZone`, replicated push-model). The checks: `Souls/SoulComponent.cpp` (`ActivateMove`, `RequestActivateMove`), `Abilities/MonsterAbilitySystemComponent.cpp` (`ApplyHit`), `Abilities/MoveProjectile.cpp` (`HandleOverlap`), `Monsters/MonsterStateTree.cpp` (the sense evaluator). Nothing here replicates beyond the two flags and the travel serial.
