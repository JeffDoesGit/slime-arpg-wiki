# Combat

*Built in roadmap item E-2.6 (branch `feat/combat-damage`). Provisional on four design memos: how a hit resolves (D-3.6), damage types (D-3.1), monsters on the player's rules (D-2.4) and player death (D-2.3).*

## What you see

Swing at a monster and, most of the time, it flinches and loses health. Sometimes you miss. Sometimes you crit and it loses more. Hit it enough and it plays its death animation and, a few seconds later, disappears. The monster does the same to you: its swings take your health down on the orb, and at zero you fall, then reappear at the start of the level with full health and mana a few seconds later. Acid Spit hurts whatever it bursts on.

Players cannot hurt each other. Monsters do not hurt monsters.

## How it works, in plain words

Every hit goes through one function on the server, the same one whether a player hits a monster or a monster hits a player. In order:

1. **Roll.** Since E-3.13 (2026-10-27) there is no accuracy and no chance to miss by default: a hit lands unless the target dodges it or the attacker is Blinded. The chance to land is (1 - Blind) x (1 - Dodge). Dodge is a chance on the target, capped at 75%, against every hit that rolls for itself (melee, projectile, area or spell, from a player or a monster; never an ailment tick or a share of another hit). Blind is a chance that the blinded attacker's hit misses (a 30% Blind misses 30% of its hits). Both are rolled pseudo-randomly, as Path of Exile rolls evasion: a counter per target (and per blinded attacker) adds the chance to land each hit and lets the hit through each time it passes 1, so 50% Dodge avoids exactly every other hit. A missed or dodged hit does nothing at all: no damage, no ailment, no on-hit effect. "Dodge" floats over the defender, "Miss" over the target of a blinded hit, seen by the player who was hit and by the player who swung. Monsters have no Dodge of their own unless their kit gives one.
2. **Crit.** A chance to crit, 5% plus the attacker's critical-chance lines, for 150% damage plus its critical-damage lines.
3. **Type.** Every hit has exactly one type: physical, fire, cold, lightning or toxic. The number is the move's damage plus the attacker's flat lines (untyped, and of the hit's type), times its increased lines summed, times its more lines compounded (since E-3.9; every red gem was inert before it).
4. **Mitigate.** Physical damage is reduced by defense on a ratio that never reaches zero (defense 100 halves it). Elemental damage is reduced by that element's resistance, capped at 75%, less the attacker's penetration of that type, never below zero.
5. **Apply.** Health goes down. At zero, the pawn dies.

Before any of that, one check: a hit on or from a pawn standing in a safe zone is not applied at all, no roll and no flinch (GDD 6.1; see [Zones](zones.md)). Moves cannot even be pressed there.

All the numbers in that list are rows of one data table, `DT_CombatRules`. Every move's damage and type are a row in a second table, `DT_MoveStats`, named after the move; a monster's health, defense and resistances are its row in a third, `DT_MonsterStats`. Only a move's reach and timing stay on the soul asset. Nothing is in code, and retuning a fight is a spreadsheet edit and a reimport, no build.

The first pass at those numbers (E-2.26) aimed for the Corpse dying in about five Venom Rake hits and a fresh level-1 player dying in about nine swings. The first Zone1 fight (2026-09-13) found two Creepy monsters unkillable by a level-1 Creepy, so the Creepy kit hits half again as hard (Venom Rake 30; since E-2.288, Jon 2026-11-12, the player's Venom Rake is 10 and the Creepy monsters keep 30 by a move override), a Creepy monster has 40 health, and a monster row now carries a **damage scale**: the monster hits with its soul's moves at that fraction of a player's damage (the Creepy monster at a fifth, the Corpse at full). Two Creepies swing every 1.3 seconds each, so at half damage they still won in nine seconds against a level-1 Corpse. One column, read off the attacker; the damage function does not ask who is hitting. They are placeholders with no design behind them, marked as such, and will move as soon as someone plays with them.

A melee move lands its hit a fixed fraction of a second into its animation, on every enemy within a short range and a cone in front of the attacker. That timing is a number on the move, not a mark on the animation, so a dedicated server that plays no animations still lands hits. A projectile lands its hit on whatever it bursts on.

Being hit plays a flinch animation, and only that: it never interrupts what you were doing. Dying plays the death animation and holds it. A monster despawns after a delay; a player respawns at the level's PlayerStart with full vitals, on the same character, so nothing in the bag or the slots is lost.

## Control ailments

**Taunt (E-2.238, 2026-10-21)** is a control of the same family: a taunted monster attacks whoever taunted it. Only the first taunt counts, and it ends when its time runs out (3 s by default) or when the taunter is gone. The Creepy's Sweet Rot node taunts monsters onto its bloom for as long as the bloom draws them. A taunted monster shows a T on its plate. Under D-3.7 like the rest (register row `D-3.7`); the research behind it is `Research Bank/2026-10-21-taunt-in-arpgs-research.md`.

**Stagger on bosses, resistance on Elites (E-2.280, 2026-11-08, provisional on D-2.32).** A boss used to shrug off every stun, freeze, knockback, root, pull and taunt, and take slows, shocks, blinds and weakens at half. Now a boss has a **stagger meter**, a thin gold bar under its life bar in the target bar and a thin line under its name plate. Every control you land on it fills the meter instead of holding it: a stun or a freeze adds 20 points a second it would have lasted, a knockback or a pull 10, a root or a taunt 8 a second, and the soft four 4 a second times their strength while still applying at half as before. Damage fills it a little too, half a point for every 1% of the boss's life you take off, so a build with no control reaches the window eventually and a control build reaches it far sooner (four 1.5 s stuns break a fresh boss). At 100 points the boss is **Staggered**: the bar turns white and drains while it reads "STAGGERED", a "!!" square joins its status row, and the boss stands stunned for 3 s, its move cut short, past any crowd-control resistance and past the 4 s in 8 s hard-control clock; "damage to stunned enemies" lines fire on it for those seconds. When the break ends the meter is empty and the boss needs a quarter more next time, up to double. A meter nobody has controlled for 4 s drains at 10 points a second, so chip damage alone never breaks a boss. A boss's row may turn its meter off or size it, and set its break's length. **Elites** are not immune and have no meter: they now spawn with 30% crowd-control resistance (a 1.5 s stun lasts 1.05 s before the Elite half, so about half a second), and take slows, shocks, blinds and weakens at three quarters. Players are untouched. Every number is a placeholder of the memo; `Slime.BossStagger 0` restores the old boss rule exactly. Register row `D-2.32`.

Since the tree-verbs pass of 2026-09-27 (E-2.123, E-2.124) a landed hit can also leave a **control ailment** on its target: a slow, a stun, a knockback or a weaken. A move's row names which one and how often (Grave Hands slows every enemy it hits), and the tree can apply one too (Reeking Shroud weakens everyone standing in the shroud). Each kind is one instance per target: a second application refreshes the clock to the full duration and keeps the stronger of the two magnitudes, so nothing stacks (a refresh never shortens: a shorter application on a longer one leaves the time that was left, since 2026-10-04); different kinds coexist. A **slow** cuts the walk speed and the swing speed by its percentage. A **stun** stops the pawn: it cannot move, cannot act, and the move it was in the middle of is cut short, its unlanded claws dropped; it is the one thing that interrupts a move. A **knockback** shoves the target a fixed distance away from the attacker in one hop; it has no duration. A **weaken** cuts the damage the target deals by its percentage. A Boss-tier monster ignores stun and knockback and takes slow and weaken at half strength. Your own tree can push back: Sure Footed and Heavy Bones reject a knockback and halve a slow or a stun; Molt clears every control on you when it triggers. The name plate shows what is on a pawn and how long it has left, read from the server. Since E-2.127 three more kinds exist on the same terms: a **freeze** stops the pawn where it stands and it cannot act, for a base time times the freeze's strength (two seconds at 1.0), but unlike a stun it does not cut short the move already in progress; a **shock** makes the pawn take that much more damage from every hit, counted after armour and resistance; a **blind** takes that much off the pawn's accuracy, so its swings miss more (never below the 5 % floor). A boss ignores a freeze as it ignores a stun, and takes shock and blind at half strength. **Crowd-control resistance** shortens every control on its bearer by its value, up to 75 %, never to nothing, and never touches a knockback; the Crowd-control resistance gem line gives it. The chance-to-freeze, shock and blind gem lines do nothing yet: no rule says how a gem's chance adds to a move's. Every number is a `DT_CombatRules` row and every rule is the D-3.7 memo's, which Duilio has not decided.

## Settled

- The server resolves every hit; your machine shows the result after the fact (GDD 7.7, 9.8).
- One function for both directions; the differences between a monster and a player are numbers on their assets, never a separate rule.
- Numbers live in `DT_CombatRules` and on the soul and monster assets.

## Hit shapes (E-2.148)

A melee move hits whatever its shape on the ground touches. There are three shapes: a cone (a wedge in front of the attacker), a circle (a disc in front of or around the attacker) and a capsule (a straight band in front, round at its far end). Each move has its own shape and sizes in the move table, so a wide sweep, a short jab and a ground slam can each be tuned on their own row. A target is hit when its body touches the shape, not only when its centre is inside. Players and monsters use the same shapes through the same code, and both walk to the distance their own shape reaches before they swing.

A move whose row sets no shape uses the reach and arc on the soul asset, as every move did before, so the moves in the game today land as they did.

For testers: `Slime.ShowHitShapes 2` draws every melee hit's shape on the ground for two seconds, green when it touched an enemy and red when it touched nobody. It is drawn where the server tests the hit, so you see it when you host or play alone, not as a joiner.

For engineers: `FHitShape` in `Abilities/HitShape.h` (`FromRow`, `Touches`, `EdgeReach`); the columns `HitShape`, `ShapeLength`, `ShapeRadius`, `ShapeAngle`, `ShapeOffset` on `FMoveStatRow`; `USoulComponent::ResolveMeleeHit` is the one caller of the test. Lengths count from the edge of the attacker's capsule, a circle's radius from its centre. The numbers are placeholders.

## Chains of triggered effects (E-2.166)

Some effects set off other effects: a hit plants a poison stack, a fracture throws Splinters onto neighbours, a kill by poison spreads the poison. Left alone, two of these can feed each other for ever. One rule stops that. Every hit, counted stack and placed area carries an **origin** and a **chain depth**.

- **Direct**: what a player or monster did on purpose. A swing, a projectile, a dash crossing, a placed area, an aura, the fracture of Marrow Rush or Tear.
- **Triggered**: what another effect set off. A poison tick, a Hive Heart debt payment, a Bone Choir fracture, the Splinters that Shrapnel and Ossuary plant.
- **Expiry**: what happens when something runs out. Brittle Bone's fracture.

A reaction that makes a further hit, stack or area fires only when its cause is Direct and the chain is shorter than the bound (3). So a retaliation answers a swing and never a poison tick, and a choir fracture does not plant Shrapnel that could set off another fracture. Three reactions are held to the bound only, because the plain rule would break what they are for: Carrion's spread when a poison tick kills, Brittle Bone on a Splinter that Shrapnel planted, and Hive Heart deferring a triggered hit. Kill credit, experience, fragments, flask charges and the heal or mana a kill gives are never asked.

Two outcomes differ from before the rule: a Bone Choir fracture plants no Shrapnel, and Carrion's copies of copies stop at depth 3.

For testers: `Slime.TriggerOriginRule 0` turns the rule off and the game behaves as it did before; `Slime.MaxHitChainDepth` sets the bound. A reaction refused by the bound warns once per source pawn in the server log; a reaction refused because its cause was not Direct says nothing.

For engineers: `EHitOrigin`, `FHitSpec::Origin`, `ChainDepth` and `MarkTriggered` in `Abilities/DamageTypes.h`; the pure rules `UMonsterAbilitySystemComponent::EvaluateTriggerAllowed` and `EvaluateChainDepthAllowed`, asked through `IsTriggerAllowed` and `IsChainDepthAllowed` at every reaction site (`Progression/SoulTreeHooks.cpp`, `Abilities/Corpse/CorpseAbilities.cpp`, `Abilities/MoveArea.cpp`, `Souls/SoulComponent.cpp`). Tests `SandboxARPG.Combat.TriggerOrigin.*`. Provisional: register row `trigger origin`.

## When a hit's reactions run (E-2.167)

A hit can set three things off on its victim: the answer to the blow itself (the flinch, Caustic Blood, Ossuary), the answer to where the blow left its health (Rigor, Molt), and the death. These used to run wherever the code happened to reach them, two of them in the middle of writing the new health. Molt could heal while the hit was still being counted, and the hit then reported a negative number.

Now the hit finishes first. It writes health, fills in its result and logs its `Hit:` line. Then its reactions run, always in the same order: the answer to the blow, then the answer to the health, then the death. The death still tells the killer's tree (Marrow Tithe, Grave Dust, Rot Feeder, Carrion) before the victim is paid out and cleared, as before. All of it happens before the hit hands its result back, so whoever dealt the hit sees a victim that is already dead when the hit killed.

If a reaction makes another hit on the same pawn, that hit finishes completely, with its own reactions, before the first hit's remaining reactions go on. A change of health that no hit made (regeneration, a flask, a heal, a dev command) is told at once, as before. Unfinished and Hive Heart shape a hit before it lands and are not part of this.

What differs from before: a hit that pushes a pawn under Molt's threshold reports its own damage, and the heal follows it; on a killing hit the answer to the blow runs before the death, where it ran after.

For testers: `Slime.DeferHitReactions 0` puts every reaction back where it ran before.

For engineers: `FHitReactionQueue` in `Abilities/HitReactionQueue.h` (`BeginHit`, `Push`, `EndHitAndDrain`, `DrainOrder`), held by `UMonsterAbilitySystemComponent`; `ApplyHit` opens a frame after its early refusals, `HandleHealthChanged` records while a frame is open, `FinishHit` is the one end of `ApplyHit` and runs the frame. One array reused for every frame, nothing allocated per hit once warm. Tests `SandboxARPG.Combat.HookQueue.*`. Provisional: register row `hook queue`; the order is this build's, the research note names none.

## The damage curve (E-2.302, 2026-11-14)

Provisional, on Jon's go ("ok all seven, may tweak later"). Your hits now keep pace with the monsters of your level. Four things changed at once:

- **Level scale.** The base of your own hit (the move's damage and your flat lines) is multiplied by a hidden factor of your character level: x1.092 a level to level 20, then x1.042 a level, to level 100 (x1.42 at level 5, x2.21 at 10, x5.32 at 20). It sits beside the ability level's factor (see [Level and growth](progression.md)). Monsters, your minions and thorns do not take it. A Normal of your level takes about 5 hits at level 20 with an unspecialised ability and the zone's gems, an Elite 14, a lair Elite 16; at levels 5 and 10 it is 6 to 7 for a Normal, since the flat line was cut.
- **Added damage effectiveness.** Flat damage from gems and passive-tree lines is added to a move at its effectiveness: the move's own damage over 10, between 0.5 and 3 (Bite 10 takes a flat line at x1, Bone Spear 18 at x1.8, a 55-damage bell at x3). So a flat line is worth the same share of a hit on a weak fast move as on a slow heavy one. A web node's own bonus damage is not scaled.
- **Flat lines shrink, crit lines grow.** The flat damage gem lines are a tenth of what they were and read with one decimal ("+1.6 damage"), so a first flat line is worth about +7.5 % to +16 % of a hit instead of +75 % to +160 % and the increased, more and crit lines matter. Critical strike chance lines are three times larger at tiers I and X, critical damage lines twice. See [Gems](gems.md).
- **Monsters.** Monster life grows x1.05 a level after level 20 (was x1.07), and the soul monsters of the Fields, Jungle and Pass stand at their zone's band (Fields 6 to 10, Jungle 11 to 15, Pass 16 to 20). See [Field monsters](field-monsters.md).

In the first off-screen fights the logged hit matched the rule to the cent at levels 3, 8, 13 and 18; armoured rows take more hits than the model (the Golem 11 to 12 at level 18), a point for the retune.

For engineers: the level scale is `Data/DT_LevelScale.csv` (`FLevelScaleRow::DamageFactor`, `scripts/gen_level_scale.py`), read by `UAbilityLevelTable::LevelScaleFactors` and `FAbilityLevelRules::EvaluateLevelScale`; `UMonsterAbilitySystemComponent::EvaluateHitLevelScale` gates it as `EvaluateHitAbilityLevel` does (a player's pawn, not an `AFieldMonster`, not `bResolved`, not `bSecondary`, names an `AbilityRow`) plus not `bThorns`, and `ApplyHit` multiplies the whole `FKitHitBonus::BaseFlatOf` by level scale x ability factor before increased, more and crit; switch `Slime.LevelScale`. ## The hit formula (E-3.20, 2026-11-14)

Provisional, on Jon's okays of the formula page, decision by decision (`docs/decisions/D-3.6.md` section 11). The order a hit goes through is now written down, and the game follows it:

- **Your side.** The move's damage plus your flat lines, then every increased line that applies (untyped, the hit's type, melee, projectile or area, the tree's) added into one pool, then each More line multiplying on its own, then one crit roll (5 % plus your crit chance lines, for 150 % plus your crit damage lines), then Weaken. Nothing else multiplies your hit.
- **Their side.** Armour reduces physical hits only, by K / (K + Armour). K now comes from a table by the attacker's level; it is 100 at every level for now, so 100 Armour still halves a physical hit. Fire, cold, lightning and toxic hits are cut by that resistance, capped at 75 %, less the attacker's penetration, never below zero.
- **Damage taken, in two steps.** First every "increased damage taken" debuff on the target adds into one pool and multiplies the hit once, held to a cap (Shock, 20 %, is the only one today; the cap is 90 %, a placeholder). Then each "less damage taken" line multiplies on its own (two 20 % lines take 64 %). Shock will be revised later: its size, cap, duration and how to counter it (row E-3.23).
- **Poison.** A poison stack deals its move's number, raised once, when it lands, by your increased damage, increased Toxic damage, ailment and damage-over-time lines. Your flat damage, your More lines and your crit never reach it, and changing your gems later does not change stacks already ticking. Before, every tick took your flat and More lines again, live.

No number moved in play except poison's: with no flat Toxic line, no More line and no tree bonus to hits, a stack deals what it did before. The server log's `Hit:` line now ends with what the target's side did to the hit, for example `[Armour 100, K 100 at attacker level 18: x0.500] [increased damage taken x1.200]`.

For engineers: K is `UCombatRules::GetArmourK` (`FArmourKRow`, `Data/DT_ArmourK.csv` written by `scripts/gen_armour_k.py`, asset made by `scripts/editor/make_armour_k_table.py`, path `ArmourKTable` in `DefaultGame.ini`; pure `EvaluateArmourK`), read at `UMonsterAbilitySystemComponent::EvaluateAttackerLevel` (a field monster's row level, a player's character level, 1 otherwise) for the physical step and the web's ElementalArmour share; switch `Slime.Damage.ArmourKByLevel`. ## Portions, conversion and tags (E-3.21, 2026-11-14)

Provisional, on Jon's okays of the formula page (`docs/decisions/D-3.6.md` section 11, decisions 1 to 3). A hit can now carry more than one damage type. Most hits still have one: a move names its type. When something says "50 % of Physical damage converted to Fire" or "gain 10 % of Physical damage as extra Lightning damage", the hit is split at its base, before any bonus, into portions, one per type:

- **Converted** damage leaves its old type: of a 120 Physical base, 50 % converted makes 60 Physical and 60 Fire. **Extra** damage is added and takes nothing away: 10 % as extra Lightning makes 120 Physical and 12 Lightning. A tree node's lines apply first, then a gem's; conversions out of one type that add past 100 % are scaled down to 100 %.
- Each portion takes only the bonuses of its own type, plus the untyped ones and those of the hit's tags (melee, projectile, area, explosive): the Fire half ignores "increased Physical damage", the Physical half ignores "increased Fire damage". More lines and the crit multiply the whole hit.
- On the target each portion meets its own defence: Armour cuts the Physical portion only, the Fire portion meets fire resistance (and the attacker's fire penetration). Shock and "less damage taken" then act once on the sum.
- You still see one number, coloured by the largest portion. A rule that asks for a hit's type (an on-hit effect of Toxic hits, say) holds when any portion is of that type.
- **Explosive is a tag, not a type**: "increased explosive damage" joins the increased bonuses of an explosive hit and has no resistance.

No soul node, gem line or monster carries a conversion yet, so nothing in play changed: with no conversion a hit computes exactly what it did (pinned by a test). The tree nodes come in CT-2.80. The page's worked example (100 Physical, +20 flat, half converted to Fire, a crit, against 100 Armour and 40 % fire resistance) lands 208.1 in the test, as the page says. The server log's `Hit:` line names every type of a split hit and gives each portion before and after its defence, for example `for 208.12 Physical+Fire crit ... [Physical 180.00 [Armour 100, K 100 at attacker level 1: x0.500] -> 90.00] [Fire 168.75 [resistance 0.40 capped 0.40 less penetration 0.10: x0.700] -> 118.12]`.

For testers (host or single player): `Slime.TestConvert Physical Lightning 50` makes your hits convert half their Physical base to Lightning as a node would (`... 10 extra` for gain-as-extra, `Slime.TestConvert clear` to remove; a pawn name after it for another pawn); `Slime.TestHitTag Explosive 1` counts your hits as explosive, and `Slime.SetAttribute ExplosiveDamageIncreased 0.3` gives the line. `Slime.Damage.Portions 0` turns the whole thing off.

## Chill, Freeze, the shatter and ailment resistance (E-3.25, 2026-11-15)

Provisional, on Jon's cards of 2026-11-15; every number is a placeholder.

- **Chill.** Every Cold hit Chills what it hits: 30 % slower movement and 30 % slower attacks and casts, for 4 seconds. One Chill at a time; a new Cold hit starts it over. A Boss is Chilled half as much and its stagger meter does not fill from it. A slow and a Chill together multiply (30 % and 30 % leave 49 % of the speed). Monsters' Cold hits Chill players the same way. The status row shows a C.
- **Freeze.** Moves and nodes Freeze as before. A Cold hit on an enemy that is already Chilled can also Freeze it: your "chance to Freeze" (0 to start; tree nodes and the gem line "#% chance to Freeze", which now rolls on gems) is rolled, and a Frozen enemy cannot move or act for 2 seconds. A Boss is never Frozen: a Freeze fills its stagger meter instead. Because every Cold hit Chills, the first Cold hit on a fresh enemy never Freezes it.
- **Shatter.** An enemy that dies while Frozen shatters: its body is gone (no corpse and no Remains for the Vetala), and every enemy of whoever Froze it within 2.5 m takes a Cold hit for 10 % of the shattered enemy's maximum health. That hit is Cold, so it Chills too. Players never shatter. (The Corpse's Fracture, its Splinter spender, is a different thing; its engine name is still Shatter.)
- **Ailment resistance.** "N % ailment resistance" makes Shock, Chill, Freeze, Burn, Bleed and Poison on you last N % shorter, up to 75 %. A Burn, Bleed or Poison that runs shorter deals its damage at the same rate, so it deals less in all. It works on players, monsters and minions alike, and it stacks with control resistance on Shock, Chill and Freeze. The character panel shows it, and your Freeze chance.

For testers (host or single player): `Slime.Kit.Op Hit Target=HitTarget Name=Target Arg=Cold Flat=20` throws a Cold hit at the nearest enemy (the log says `gained chill`, then `refreshed chill`); `Slime.SetAttribute FreezeChance 1` makes the next Cold hit on a Chilled enemy Freeze it (`Cold: ... freezes it`); kill a Frozen enemy to see `Shatter: ... died Frozen and shatters`; `Slime.ApplyControl chill` Chills your own pawn (`Slime.ShowControls` lists it). ## One Bleed and one Burn (E-3.26, 2026-11-15)

Provisional, on Jon's cards of 2026-11-15; every number is a placeholder.

- **Bleed.** A Bleed deals 60 % of the Physical base damage of the hit that applied it every second, for 6 seconds, as Physical damage over time. Moving or standing still does not change it. "Base damage" is the hit's damage before any increased or more line and before its crit, after conversion: a hit with no Physical in it applies no Bleed.
- **Burn.** A Burn deals 90 % of the Fire base damage of the hit that applied it every second, for 4 seconds, as Fire damage over time. A Physical hit converted in part to Fire burns from its Fire part.
- **Scaling.** As it is applied, the Bleed or Burn is raised once by the lines that reach damage over time: increased damage, increased damage of its type, increased damage over time, the ailment's own lines from nodes, and more damage over time. It keeps that number while it runs. The hit's crit never raises it, and lines that only reach hits (melee, projectile, area, more damage on hits) do not either.
- **One at a time.** An enemy carries one Bleed and one Burn, whoever applied them. A new one that deals more each second takes over and runs its full time; a new one that deals the same or less does nothing (the one already burning is not shortened or replaced). Poison is unchanged: it stacks.
- **The Vampire.** Each hit of the Vampire on an enemy carrying its Bleed sets that Bleed back to its full time, at the damage it already deals; a fast hitter does not deal more for hitting fast.
- **Ailment resistance** shortens both, as before (E-3.25).

For testers (host or single player): `Slime.Kit.Op ApplyAilment Target=HitTarget Name=Bleed Flat=600` lays a Bleed worth 600 on the nearest enemy (the log says `Ailment: ... +1 Bleed ... over 6.0 s`); the same with `Flat=300` says `is weaker and dropped (stronger stays)`, with `Flat=1200` `gives way to the stronger`.
